How the prediction works

A transparent scoring model. Every factor adds points to a delay score, and the score becomes the delay chance.

What adds to the score

WeatherUp to 35 points
Airport trafficUp to 25 points
Inbound flight delayUp to 20 points

Reading the result

Delay chance. The total score, kept between 2% and 98%. Below 35% is low risk, 35% to 65% is medium and above 65% is high.

Expected delay. Derived from the chance, with extra minutes when the aircraft is already running late.

Score stability. How far the score sits from the nearest risk boundary. A score close to 35% or 65% could easily fall into the next risk level, so stability is lower there. It is not a statistical confidence interval.

Time of day. Departures during the morning (06:00–09:59) and evening (17:00–21:59) peaks get 8 points, midday hours get 3, and late night gets none.

Good to know. The main score is a rule-based estimate built for learning. Weather and wind can be filled from a live forecast; airport traffic is an estimate and the inbound delay is entered by you. It does not use live airline feeds.
